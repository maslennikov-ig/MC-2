# Deep Research: Должностные инструкции нового поколения

> Промпты для Deep Research. Цель — собрать лучшие мировые практики для создания wow-уровня должностных инструкций, применимых к ЛЮБОЙ должности (продажи, IT, HR, финансы, операции, маркетинг, производство и т.д.).

---

## Промпт 1: Структура и wow-элементы

```
Research the most innovative, modern, and impressive ROLE GUIDE / POSITION PLAYBOOK formats used by top companies worldwide. I'm NOT looking for job postings or vacancy ads — I'm looking for comprehensive internal documents that fully define any role for business owners and managers.

IMPORTANT: The research must be UNIVERSAL — applicable to ANY role in any department (sales, engineering, marketing, HR, finance, operations, logistics, production, customer support, legal, etc.). Don't focus on one function — find patterns that work across all roles.

WHAT I'M BUILDING:
A universal "Role Guide" generator that produces comprehensive documents for any position. A business owner receives it and says "wow, this is exactly what I needed to manage this role." Current structure has 18 sections including: role purpose, KPIs, duties by frequency, competencies, tools, onboarding 30-60-90, motivation, processes, red flags, career path, industry context, business impact.

RESEARCH THESE SPECIFIC AREAS:

1. **Structure innovations — what sections do the BEST role guides include that most companies miss?**
   Look at real examples:
   - GitLab's public handbook — role definitions for engineering, marketing, sales, HR
   - Basecamp/37signals — "Shape Up", role ownership concepts
   - Netflix culture deck — context over control, role freedom & responsibility
   - Valve employee handbook — flat structure, self-directed roles
   - Buffer's transparent role guides and salary formulas
   - Stripe's operating manuals
   - Bridgewater (Ray Dalio) — role mapping, "baseball cards" for people
   - Spotify's squad model — how roles are defined in autonomous teams
   - Amazon's leadership principles tied to role expectations
   - Toyota's standardized work documents for operational roles

   What SECTIONS or CONCEPTS appear in the best guides that typical companies skip entirely?

2. **"Wow" elements that make a role guide extraordinary — UNIVERSAL, not role-specific:**
   - "Anti-goals" — what this role should explicitly NOT be doing (prevents scope creep)
   - Decision-making authority matrix — what can this person decide alone vs. need approval?
   - "Definition of Done" for each responsibility — how do you know the task is truly complete?
   - Failure modes — what does failure look like in this role? What are the 3 most common ways people fail?
   - "First 5 wins" — quick wins any new hire should achieve regardless of role
   - Energy map — which tasks in this role drain energy vs. energize (helps with hiring fit)
   - Role dependencies — who is BLOCKED if this person doesn't deliver?
   - "If I'm hit by a bus" — what must be documented for continuity?
   - Communication charter — how this role communicates (async vs sync, which channels, response times)
   - Cognitive load mapping — how many parallel tracks this role juggles
   - Autonomy spectrum — which decisions are fully autonomous, which need alignment, which need approval
   - "Superpowers needed" — the 1-2 things where this person must be exceptional (not just "good at everything")
   - Time allocation pie chart — ideal % split across responsibilities
   - Stakeholder map — who cares about this role's output and why

3. **Modern frameworks for role definition (applicable to any function):**
   - OKR-based role definitions — role tied to company OKRs
   - "Job Scorecard" from "Who: The A Method for Hiring" (Geoff Smart)
   - Topgrading scorecard methodology
   - RACI/RAPID matrices for accountability
   - Holacracy role definitions (purpose, domains, accountabilities)
   - Traction/EOS Accountability Chart + GWC (Get it, Want it, Capacity)
   - "Manager of One" concept (Basecamp)
   - "Tours of Duty" (Reid Hoffman) — time-boxed missions within a role
   - Progression frameworks: Dropbox, Spotify, Buffer, CircleCI (publicly available)
   - Competency matrices / skills ladders used at Google, Meta, Stripe

4. **Books and thought leaders to mine:**
   - "Who: The A Method for Hiring" (Geoff Smart) — Job Scorecard
   - "The E-Myth Revisited" (Michael Gerber) — position agreements, systemizing any role
   - "Traction" (Gino Wickman) — accountability charts, GWC, Rocks
   - "Work Rules!" (Laszlo Bock) — how Google defines and hires for roles
   - "Powerful" (Patty McCord) — Netflix's approach to role context
   - "The Alliance" (Reid Hoffman) — tours of duty
   - "High Output Management" (Andy Grove) — output-oriented role thinking
   - "Principles" (Ray Dalio) — role mapping, baseball cards, believability
   - "Drive" (Dan Pink) — autonomy/mastery/purpose in role design
   - "Radical Candor" (Kim Scott) — role clarity, care personally + challenge directly
   - "The Checklist Manifesto" (Atul Gawande) — checklists for any role
   - "Turn the Ship Around!" (David Marquet) — leader-leader model for role autonomy
   - "Measure What Matters" (John Doerr) — OKRs applied to individual roles
   - Patrick Lencioni "The Ideal Team Player" — humble, hungry, smart as universal role fit

5. **Visual and format innovations:**
   - One-page role canvas (like Business Model Canvas but for a role)
   - Role "baseball cards" (Bridgewater approach)
   - Competency radar/spider charts
   - Career progression maps (visual, not just text)
   - Decision trees for common role scenarios
   - Stakeholder relationship diagrams
   - Time allocation donut/pie charts
   - Responsibility landscape maps

DELIVERABLE:
A prioritized list of the TOP 25 most impressive "wow" elements for a universal role guide. For each:
- What it is (clear definition)
- Why it impresses (what's the "aha moment" for a business owner?)
- Which roles it applies to (all? managers only? client-facing only?)
- Real company example if available
- How to implement in a markdown document

Prioritize elements that:
a) Work for ANY role (universal)
b) Are actionable for business owners (not HR jargon)
c) Haven't been widely adopted yet (novelty factor)
d) Can be generated by AI from a role description (automatable)
```

## Промпт 2: Операционные элементы и практические фреймворки

```
Research the most effective OPERATIONAL PLAYBOOK elements for employee role guides — focusing on KPI systems, onboarding, checklists, performance signals, and motivation frameworks. Everything must be UNIVERSAL — applicable to any role in any department.

CONTEXT:
I'm building a universal role guide generator. The output document must be so actionable that a business owner can hand it to a new hire on Day 1 and say "this is your playbook — follow it and you'll succeed." The guide covers all roles: engineers, marketers, salespeople, accountants, warehouse managers, HR, designers, support agents, operations, legal — ANYTHING.

RESEARCH THESE AREAS IN DEPTH:

1. **KPI frameworks that work across ALL roles:**
   - Leading vs. lagging indicators — how to define "input metrics" (what you control) vs "output metrics" (what you measure) for ANY role
   - "North Star Metric" per role — the ONE number that matters most. How to identify it for:
     * Revenue roles (sales, account management)
     * Support roles (customer success, support)
     * Creative roles (design, content, marketing)
     * Technical roles (engineering, DevOps, QA)
     * Operational roles (logistics, warehouse, production)
     * Administrative roles (HR, finance, legal)
   - "Traffic light" KPI systems — green/yellow/red thresholds with specific ACTIONS for each zone (not just colors)
   - "Scorecard" approach (Topgrading) — how to build a 5-7 metric scorecard for any position
   - Anti-patterns: what NOT to measure and why (Goodhart's Law in practice)
   - How Amazon, Google, Spotify, Toyota define individual KPIs
   - Andy Grove's "High Output Management" — pairing activity metrics with output metrics
   - "Balanced Scorecard" adapted for individual roles (not just company level)

2. **Onboarding innovations (30-60-90 and beyond) — UNIVERSAL patterns:**
   - Ramp-up curves: how to define "what good looks like" at day 7, 14, 30, 60, 90 for ANY role
   - "Learning sprints" — 2-week focused learning blocks with clear deliverables
   - Onboarding scorecards with pass/fail criteria (not just "learn the product")
   - Self-assessment checklists for new hires — "rate yourself 1-5 on these skills"
   - "Graduation" milestones — when is onboarding officially complete? What's the test?
   - The "buddy vs mentor vs manager" triangle — who does what during onboarding
   - Knowledge checkpoints — not just reading, but demonstrating understanding
   - Real examples: Google Noogler, Shopify, Zapier (remote), GitLab (handbook-first), Basecamp
   - How to adapt onboarding for different role types (highly technical vs. relationship-based vs. operational)

3. **Process and checklist design — FOR ANY ROLE:**
   - "The Checklist Manifesto" (Atul Gawande) — DO-CONFIRM vs. READ-DO checklist types. When to use which?
   - When to use: checklists vs. decision trees vs. flowcharts vs. playbooks vs. SOPs
   - How McDonald's, Toyota, Amazon design SOPs that work for ANY role level
   - "If this, then that" decision frameworks — how to handle the 10 most common situations in any role
   - Escalation matrices — when and how to escalate (universal pattern, not role-specific)
   - Process triggers — what starts a process, what ends it, what's the output
   - "Exception handling" — what to do when the standard process doesn't apply
   - Communication protocols: async vs sync, response time expectations, status update formats

4. **Red flags and early warning systems — UNIVERSAL patterns:**
   - Leading indicators of failure BY ROLE CATEGORY:
     * Knowledge workers: declining output quality, missed deadlines, reduced collaboration
     * Client-facing: declining satisfaction scores, complaint escalation, avoidance behaviors
     * Technical: increasing bug rate, skipping code reviews, avoiding complex tasks
     * Operational: rising error rate, safety incidents, declining throughput
     * Managerial: team attrition, skipped 1-on-1s, decisions bottleneck
   - "Smell tests" — qualitative signals that something is wrong (universal)
   - The 5 stages of disengagement (research from Gallup, Harvard Business Review)
   - "Stay interviews" — preventive questions before problems escalate
   - "Keeper test" (Netflix) — would you fight to keep this person?
   - Performance Improvement Plan alternatives that actually work
   - Continuous feedback frameworks (not just annual reviews): 15Five, Lattice, Culture Amp approaches

5. **Motivation and retention — what ACTUALLY works by role type:**
   - Dan Pink's Autonomy/Mastery/Purpose — how to apply to different role types:
     * High-autonomy roles (senior engineers, creatives)
     * Process-driven roles (operations, support, finance)
     * Results-driven roles (sales, business development)
     * People-driven roles (HR, management, coaching)
   - "Career conversations" framework (Russ Laraway, Google) — 3 questions for any role
   - Total Rewards beyond salary — what actually motivates per role category
   - Gamification that works (not cringe): meaningful milestones, skill badges, progression visibility
   - "Tours of Duty" (Reid Hoffman) — time-boxed 2-4 year missions with clear transformation goals
   - Progression frameworks from Spotify (engineering + non-engineering), Dropbox, Buffer, CircleCI
   - Intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation — which levers for which roles
   - "Job crafting" — allowing employees to reshape their role within boundaries

6. **Industry context and trends — how to keep role guides current:**
   - How to write an "industry context" section that ages well (principles, not facts)
   - Identifying which trends actually affect a specific role vs. noise
   - "Future-proofing" a role — what skills will matter in 2-3 years
   - AI impact by role category — what gets automated, what becomes more valuable
   - How to structure a "continuous learning" section that's actionable, not generic

DELIVERABLE:
For each of the 6 areas, provide TOP 5 most actionable innovations. For each innovation:
- What it is (1-2 sentences)
- Why it's better than the traditional approach
- Which role types it works best for (or "all")
- Real company example if available
- How to implement it in a text/markdown-based role guide

PRIORITY: Things that make a business owner think "I've never seen this in a job description before — this changes how I think about managing this role." Focus on UNIVERSAL patterns, not role-specific tricks.
```
